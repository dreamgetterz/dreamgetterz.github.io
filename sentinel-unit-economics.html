Reading the number

What would actually break this

Re-analysis loops. A single pass over a transcript is cheap. An agent that re-reads the full transcript on every tool call multiplies the input tokens by the number of turns, and that is the one line item here that can silently grow 10×. Cap it with a fixed pass count and prompt caching, not with hope.

Streaming instead of batch. Real-time ASR runs roughly 30–50% above pre-recorded rates, and the Batch API's 50% discount is off the table if analysis has to be live. Live coaching during the call is a different product with a different floor — price it separately.

Per-minute telephony. Nothing above includes carrier or platform minutes. If Sentinel records through your own voice stack rather than ingesting the dialer's audio, add that rate per audio hour before comparing to the ceiling — it is typically larger than transcription and model cost combined.

Seat drift. The ceiling is price ÷ hours. A client who adds reps without a price change moves the ceiling down proportionally. Tie the contract to seats or to an audio-hour band, not to a flat number.

The lever you already have. Relief One's own data shows 95 of 160 inbound calls run over three minutes — the other 65 are wrong numbers, hangups, and misroutes carrying almost no talk time and no gradeable content. A duration floor before transcription costs you nothing in coverage and trims the ASR bill. It is a smaller saving than it looks (short calls are short) but it is free.

Model rates are Anthropic list prices per million tokens; Sonnet 5 introductory pricing runs through 31 Aug 2026. Transcription, storage, and infrastructure figures are market-rate placeholders — replace them with your contracted rates for a binding number.
